Thai alphabet for beginners

The Thai alphabet looks hard at first, but it is very regular. Here is what you are really learning and a calm order to learn it in.

What is in the Thai alphabet?

  • 44 consonant letters. They stand for fewer sounds, because some sounds have more than one letter.
  • Vowels. Thai vowels are written before, after, above or below the consonant. There are about 32 vowel forms, but you meet only a few at a time.
  • 4 tone marks and 5 tones. The tone of a word comes from the consonant, the vowel length and the tone mark together.

A good order to learn it

1. Start with a few easy letters. Learn four or five consonants and two vowels, then read real words straight away. In Lesson 1 of Thai From Zero you learn ก ด ต ป with า and ี, and you can already read กา, ตา and ดี.

2. Say the word out loud every time. Thai has no spaces between words and the letters do not always sound like their English cousins. Listen to a native speaker first, then read.

3. Add letters in small groups. Five new letters a week is a good pace. Review old letters inside new words.

4. Learn the tones early. Do not leave tones for later. A word with the wrong tone can be a different word. Read our guide to the 5 Thai tones.

5. Learn the three consonant classes. Consonants are mid, high or low class. The class helps you predict the tone when you read. You do not need this on day one, but you will need it for reading signs and menus.

Common questions

How long does it take to learn the Thai alphabet?

Most learners can recognise all the consonants in a few weeks with ten minutes of practice a day. Reading smoothly takes longer, and that is normal.

Do I need to learn to read Thai?

You can speak without it, but reading helps your pronunciation a lot. Romanised spelling is not consistent, so letters give you a steadier base.
