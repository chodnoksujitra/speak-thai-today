Thai tones explained

Thai has five tones. Say the same sound with a different tone and you may say a different word. Here is a plain guide with examples.

The 5 Thai tones

Each tone is a different pitch shape. Try them on the sound maa:

Mid toneflat, normal voiceมาmaacome
Low toneflat and lowerใหม่màinew
Falling tonestarts high, dropsไม่mâinot
High tonehigher, a little risingม้าmáahorse
Rising tonestarts low, goes upหมาmǎadog

Another good set, on the sound kaao:

Lowข่าวkàaonews
Fallingข้าวkâaorice
Risingขาวkǎaowhite

Why tones matter

In English, a rising voice can turn a sentence into a question. In Thai, the pitch is part of the word itself. Learn each new word together with its tone, the way you learn its spelling.

How to practise the tones

  1. Listen first

    Hear a native speaker say a word several times before you copy it.

  2. Practise pairs

    Say two words that differ only in tone, like ม้า and หมา, and hear the difference.

  3. Use your hand

    Draw the pitch in the air: flat, up, down. It helps your ear and your voice agree.

  4. Do not use a tune

    The tone shape matters, not how high your voice is. Men, women and children all use the same shapes.

Where do tones come from in writing?

The tone is set by four things together. You will learn this step by step.

  • Class of the first consonant mid, high or low
  • Open or closed syllable
  • Vowel length
  • Tone mark if there is one
