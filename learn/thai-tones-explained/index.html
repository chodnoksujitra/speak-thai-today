Thai tones explained

Thai has five tones. Say the same sound with a different tone and you may say a different word. Here is a plain guide with examples.

The 5 Thai tones

Each tone is a different pitch shape. Try them on the sound maa:

  • Mid tone (flat, normal voice): มา maa = come
  • Low tone (flat and lower): ใหม่ mài = new
  • Falling tone (starts high, drops): ไม่ mâi = not
  • High tone (higher, a little rising): ม้า máa = horse
  • Rising tone (starts low, goes up): หมา mǎa = dog

Another good set: ข่าว kàao (news), ข้าว kâao (rice) and ขาว kǎao (white).

Why tones matter

In English, a rising voice can turn a sentence into a question. In Thai, the pitch is part of the word itself. Learn each new word together with its tone, the way you learn its spelling.

How to practise the tones

1. Listen first. Hear a native speaker say a word several times before you copy it.

2. Practise pairs. Say two words that differ only in tone, like ม้า and หมา, and hear the difference.

3. Use your hand. Draw the pitch in the air: flat, up, down. It helps your ear and your voice agree.

4. Do not use a tune. The tone shape matters, not how high your voice is. Men, women and children all use the same shapes.

Where do tones come from in writing?

The tone is set by the class of the first consonant (mid, high or low), whether the syllable is open or closed, the vowel length, and the tone mark if there is one. You will learn this step by step. See also the Thai alphabet for beginners.
