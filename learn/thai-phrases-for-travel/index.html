Thai phrases for travel

You do not need much Thai to enjoy a trip. These 12 short phrases cover greetings, shops, food and taxis. Say them slowly and smile.

Before you start

Thai uses polite endings. A man ends a sentence with ครับ kráp, for statements and questions. A woman uses ค่ะ kâ for statements and คะ ká for questions. Add them to every phrase below to sound polite: short phrases without them can sound abrupt. The marks over the letters show the tone. Read about them in our guide to Thai tones.

Greetings

  • สวัสดี sà-wàt-dii = hello / goodbye. Add ครับ or ค่ะ to be polite.
  • ขอบคุณ kɔ̀ɔp-kun = thank you.

Shopping

  • อันนี้เท่าไหร่ an-níi tâo-rài = How much is this?
  • แพงไปหน่อย pɛɛng bpai nɔ̀i = A bit too expensive.
  • ลดหน่อยได้ไหมคะ lót nɔ̀i dâai-mǎi ká = Can you lower the price a little? (a woman says คะ, a man says ครับ). Bargain only at markets and street stalls, not in shops with fixed prices.
  • ไม่เอาถุง mâi ao tǔng = No bag, thanks.

Food and drink

  • เอาอันนี้ ao an-níi = I'll take this one (point at the menu).
  • ไม่เผ็ด mâi pèt = Not spicy.
  • ขอน้ำเปล่า kɔ̌ɔ náam-bplàao = Plain water, please.

Getting around

  • ห้องน้ำอยู่ที่ไหน hɔ̂ng-náam yùu tîi-nǎi = Where is the toilet?
  • เปิดมิเตอร์ได้ไหม bpə̀ət mí-dtə̂ə dâai-mǎi = Can you turn on the meter? (taxi) Loanwords like mí-dtə̂ə do not always follow the tone rules.

Tips for using them

Point and smile. Show the Thai on your phone or on the printed card and say it out loud. People understand effort.

Keep it short. One small phrase is better than a long sentence you are unsure of.
